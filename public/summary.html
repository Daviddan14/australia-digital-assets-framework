A few paragraphs

General information, not legal advice. Australia’s Digital Assets Framework Act starts on 8 April 202714. It turns two kinds of custody business into licensed financial products: digital asset platforms, which hold tokens for clients, and tokenised custody platforms, which hold a real asset and create one token for it1.

Businesses already running one can keep going if they apply for a licence by 7 October 20274. Small digital asset platforms are exempt if they hold no financial products, handle no more than $10 million of transactions a year, keep each client’s holdings within $5,000 (valued when tokens are added), and notify ASIC1.

For issuers, a fund that keeps its primary register of unit holders on chain would usually be a managed investment scheme on ASIC’s draft view, not a tokenised custody platform5. The new platform rules bite hardest on whoever holds investors’ tokens or assets.

Redbelly’s identity and eligibility tools fit the Act’s demand for transparent, non-discriminatory eligibility rules110. Whether Redbelly counts as public digital token infrastructure, which sits outside licensing, is not settled: ASIC’s draft says networks where only select persons can run consensus are not, and Redbelly selects its node operators413. ASIC is taking feedback until 27 November 20263.
