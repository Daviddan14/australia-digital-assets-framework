Digital asset platforms and

October 2026

About this guide
This guide is for operators of digital asset platforms (DAPs) and tokenised
custody platforms (TCPs), and other persons that provide financial services
in relation to those products.
It explains how the law applies to DAPs and TCPs, how to apply for or vary
your AFS licence, and your obligations as a licensee.

Note: This is a draft regulatory guide for consultation, based on the law as it is expected to
be on 8 April 2027. It seeks to implement the Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets
Framework) Act 2026 (DAF Act) and refers to regulatory design work that is being
developed by Government in parallel to this process. This is intended to enable industry to
see a complete picture of the future approach.

A

Overview

Key points
The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 (DAF
Act) introduces two new financial products: digital asset platforms (DAPs)
and tokenised custody platforms (TCPs).
Operators and other persons providing financial services in relation to
DAPs and TCPs may need an Australian financial services (AFS) licence.
AFS licensees must comply with the obligations applying to their activities.
This guide explains how the DAP and TCP framework applies, how to
apply for or vary an AFS licence, and the obligations applying to licensees
and platform operators.
Information Sheet 225 Digital assets: Financial products and services
(INFO 225) explains when digital assets and related arrangements may be
financial products.

Who this guide is for
RG 000.1

This guide is for:
(a)

operators of DAPs and TCPs;

(b)

persons applying for, or varying, an AFS licence to provide financial
services in relation to a DAP or TCP;

(c)

existing AFS licensees providing those services; and

(d)

other persons that provide, or propose to provide, financial services in
relation to a DAP or TCP.

Purpose of this guide
RG 000.2

The purpose of this guide is to:
(a)

help you determine whether your facility/platform is a DAP or TCP and
whether you need an AFS licence;

(b)

explain how to apply for an AFS licence;

(c)

explain key AFS licence obligations and where to find guidance on
these obligations; and

(d)

provide guidance on specific obligations (including rules and standards)
for DAP and TCP operators.

The DAP and TCP regime
RG 000.5

Digital tokens and digital assets are increasingly used for investment,
trading, payments, fundraising and digital representation of financial and
non-financial assets. Some financial services businesses are using digital
tokens and related infrastructure to provide services traditionally performed
through account-based or centralised arrangements.

RG 000.6

People may interact directly with digital assets through blockchains and
distributed ledger technology. Intermediaries also provide services that allow
clients to buy, sell, trade, hold or use digital assets.

RG 000.7

The DAF Act amends the Corporations Act 2001 (the Corporations Act). In
this guide, we refer to the package of changes made by the DAF Act as the
DAP and TCP regime. This regime:
(a)

introduces DAPs and TCPs as new financial products;

(b)

requires DAP and TCP operators, and certain other service providers, to
hold an AFS licence, unless an exemption applies, and requires
licensees to comply with the obligations applying to their financial
services business;

(c)

extends the meaning of dealing in a financial product to include
specified conduct by a client under a DAP or TCP;

(d)

introduces specific requirements for DAP and TCP operators, including
tailored disclosure and platform rule requirements;

(e)

provides for ASIC-made asset-holding standards and transactional and
settlement standards that apply to DAP and TCP operators;

(f)

clarifies how DAPs and TCPs interact with other financial products,
financial markets and clearing and settlement (CS) facilities;

(g)

provides Ministerial powers affecting the regulatory treatment of DAPs,
financial markets and CS facilities, and conduct involving specified
financial products through DAPs and TCPs; and

(h)

introduces targeted exemptions and exclusions for specified DAPs,
TCPs, digital assets and certain related arrangements.

RG 000.8

If you are an operator of a DAP or TCP, you will need an AFS licence to
issue the DAP or TCP. You must also have any relevant authorisations for
the financial services you provide in relation to your DAP or TCP and comply
with obligations in the Corporations Act and standards imposed by ASIC.

RG 000.9

The Explanatory Memorandum to the Corporations Amendment (Digital
Assets Framework) Bill 2025 (Explanatory Memorandum) provides further
background on the purpose and intended operation of the regime, including
detailed explanations and examples relating to digital tokens, possession,
DAPs and TCPs. This guide identifies relevant parts of the Explanatory
Memorandum where they may assist in understanding the operation of
provisions.

Determining whether the DAP and TCP regime applies
RG 000.10

Whether a platform is a DAP or TCP depends on how it operates. Broadly:
(a)

a DAP involves an operator possessing underlying assets (which are
digital tokens) for or on behalf of clients; and

(b)

a TCP involves an operator holding identified underlying assets (which
may be traditional or digital, financial or non-financial, other than

money) and creating a single digital token for each asset that confers a
right on the holder of the digital token to redeem, or direct delivery of,
that asset.
RG 000.11

Whether a facility is a DAP or TCP depends on whether the relevant
electronic records are digital tokens, who possesses or holds the underlying
assets, and for whose benefit. If a facility meets both definitions, it is treated
as a TCP rather than a DAP.

RG 000.12

A DAP or TCP may also involve other financial products or services. You
must separately consider whether the underlying assets of a DAP or TCP are
financial products, whether another regulatory regime applies, what financial
services are provided in relation to the platform or those assets, and whether
an AFS licence or licensing exemption applies.

RG 000.13

Section B provides information for working out if your facility is a DAP or
TCP and whether you need an AFS licence.

Applying for or varying an AFS licence
RG 000.14

If you need an AFS licence, you must apply for authorisations covering all
the financial services you intend to provide. A DAP or TCP operator needs
the relevant issue authorisation. Depending on its business model and
activities, an operator or other service provider may also need authorisations
for using a platform on behalf of their clients, arranging for another person
to deal in a platform, providing financial product advice, or providing
services in relation to the underlying assets of the platform that are
themselves financial products.

RG 000.15

Section C explains the licence authorisations that may be required by DAP
and TCP operators and other service providers, and our approach to assessing
licence applications. This includes how we assess organisational competence.

Your AFS licence obligations
RG 000.16

AFS licensees providing financial services in relation to DAPs and TCPs
must comply with the general obligations and other requirements that apply
to their authorisations, services and circumstances. These may include
requirements relating to financial resources, client money and property,
conflicts management, dispute resolution, compensation, disclosure, advice,
design and distribution, advertising and hawking, and breach reporting.

RG 000.17

Section D provides a catalogue of key obligations and links to relevant
legislation and ASIC guidance. The catalogue is not exhaustive. Licensees
remain responsible for identifying and complying with all obligations that
apply to their financial services business.

Specific obligations for DAP and TCP operators
RG 000.18

DAP and TCP operators must comply with additional requirements applying
specifically to their platform. For platforms issued to retail clients, these
include giving clients a DAP/TCP Guide and maintaining a voting policy.
Operators must also maintain adequate platform rules governing activities
and conduct in relation to the platform.

RG 000.19

Additionally, operators must comply with the asset-holding standards and,
where relevant to the financial services they provide, the transactional and
settlement standards. Guidance on these additional, operator-specific
requirements are set out in Section E.

RG 000.20

A DAP or TCP is a financial product, separate from any digital asset or
underlying asset made available or held through the DAP or TCP.

RG 000.21

Financial services obligations may therefore apply both to services provided

asset or underlying asset that is itself a financial product. Section B explains

each.

INFO 225 explains when a digital asset or related arrangement may be a

Corporations Act. It also explains how the DAF Act affects the treatment of
public digital token infrastructure, wrapped tokens and custodial staking

B

Determining whether the DAP and TCP regime
applies

Key points
DAPs and TCPs are financial products. This means that a person who
provides financial services in relation to a DAP or TCP must generally hold
an AFS licence covering those services, unless an exemption applies.
Whether a facility is a DAP or TCP depends on how it operates.
The concepts of digital token, possession and holding assets for or on
behalf of another person, are central to the DAP and TCP definitions. The
Corporations Act does not define the term ‘digital asset’.
A DAP or TCP may also have features of another financial product or
facility. This means that, in some cases, different requirements will apply.
This section outlines key questions and guidance to help you determine
whether the DAP and TCP regime applies.

Is the electronic record a digital token?
RG 000.27

To determine whether a facility is a DAP or TCP, you first need to consider
whether an electronic record is a digital token that the facility operator
possesses or creates. It is therefore important to understand the concepts of
‘digital token’.

Digital token
RG 000.28

A digital token is an electronic record that one or more persons are capable
of factually controlling, or ‘possessing’: see s86 and 761GB(1).

RG 000.29

A person factually controls an electronic record if they can transfer it,
exclude one or more other persons from transferring it, and demonstrate that
they can do those things: see s761GB(2). The test depends on what the
person can in fact control, rather than their legal rights or obligations in
relation to the electronic record. It does not matter that another person can
also factually control the record: see s761GB(3).
Note: Notes 1–4 to s761GB of the Corporations Act and Examples 1.1–1.6 in the
Explanatory Memorandum illustrate how the meaning of digital token applies to
different electronic records, including records that are merely information and cannot be
factually controlled.

Possessing a digital token
RG 000.30

A person possesses a digital token if they are capable of factually controlling
the relevant electronic record: s86(2)(a). However, a person does not jointly
possess the token if they need another person’s express cooperation to
exercise factual control and that other person can exercise the relevant
control unilaterally: s86(3).
Note: Examples 1.7–1.17 in the Explanatory Memorandum illustrate possession and
joint possession, including where persons have different capabilities to control the same
digital token.

Digital asset
RG 000.31

The Corporations Act does not define the term ‘digital asset’. In general, a
digital asset is the combination of a digital token and the rights and benefits
that accrue to its holder.

Is the facility a TCP?
RG 000.32

A facility meets the definition of a TCP if:
(a)

its operator identifies and holds one or more underlying assets, other
than money, for or on behalf of another person (this includes where the

operator acts as trustee or bailee or is obliged to ensure that the asset is
dealt with on that person’s instructions); and
(b)

for each underlying asset, the operator creates a single digital token
where possession of the token gives a right to redeem, or direct the
delivery of, the underlying asset: see s761GD(1).
Note: The operator is the issuer of the platform: see RG 000.8.

RG 000.33

Underlying assets of a TCP can include financial products and can include
digital tokens that are financial products. This is discussed further at
RG 000.46 and Table 5.
Note: A TCP is one way assets may be tokenised. Not every tokenisation arrangement is
a TCP. Tokenisation generally involves representing assets as digital tokens that can be
stored, transferred or traded electronically.

RG 000.34

If the operator, or another person authorised by the facility, engages in
conduct relating to an underlying asset on behalf of a client who possesses
the corresponding digital token, it is still considered to be a TCP: see
s761GD(2). The operator or other person engaging in this conduct therefore
needs an AFS licence.

RG 000.35

Not every arrangement that tokenises or digitally represents an already
existing asset is a TCP. For example, an arrangement may not be a TCP
where:
(a)

the token represents an interest in a mixed pool or combination of
assets, rather than a separately identified underlying asset type (e.g. a
token that represents a claim to 1 gram of gold and 1 kg of silver is not
a TCP);

(b)

the operator makes investment decisions for the client (as it would not
meet the requirement for the s9(md)(ii) exclusion from the definition of
a managed investment scheme);

(c)

the token represents a fraction of an asset that cannot be delivered in
that quantity (e.g. a fraction of a piece of real estate) (as it would not
meet the requirement for the s9(md)(v) exclusion from the definition of
a managed investment scheme);

(d)

the operator does not create a single digital token for each underlying
asset (s761GD(1)(b)(i)); or

(e)

the arrangement is a managed investment scheme (except for the
exemptions set out in s9(md)), derivative or another financial product:
see RG 000.49.
Note: Also see Examples 1.19–1.22 in the Explanatory Memorandum of facilities that
are and are not TCPs. For example, a ‘direct’ or ‘native’ tokenisation arrangement
(where each token represents a unit in a unit trust using the blockchain as the primary
unit-holder register) would generally be a managed investment scheme rather than a
TCP.

Is the facility a DAP?
RG 000.36

A facility meets the definition of a DAP if its operator possesses one or more
digital tokens for or on behalf of a client or the client’s nominee: see
s761GC(1)–(2). This includes where the operator acts as trustee or bailee or
is obliged to deal with the tokens on the other person’s instructions.
Note: The operator is the issuer of the platform: see RG 000.8.

RG 000.37

Merely possessing digital tokens does not make a facility a DAP—the
operator must possess them for or on behalf of a client or their nominee.

RG 000.38

If the operator, or another person authorised by the facility, holds the digital
tokens on behalf of the client or nominee, it is still considered to be a DAP:
s761GC(3). The operator or other person engaging in this conduct therefore
needs an AFS licence.

RG 000.39

DAPs may operate under different business models, depending on the assetholding, transactional and settlement functions provided by the operator. For
example, an operator may simply hold assets for its clients, or it may provide
additional services by facilitating the buying, selling, swapping or other use
of digital assets (e.g. staking or lending).

RG 000.40

The DAP operator’s business model will affect the AFS licence
authorisations it must apply for and the information it must provide with its
application. For guidance on applying for or varying an AFS licence,
including the authorisations relevant to each business model, see Section C.

RG 000.41

Not every arrangement that involves holding digital tokens is a DAP. For
example, an arrangement may not be a DAP where it:

RG 000.42

(a)

involves lending or management of collateral under which title to the
tokens is transferred and the recipient is not required to deal with them
on the other person’s instructions: see paragraph 1.85 of the
Explanatory Memorandum and the anticipated regulations (subject to
Government processes);

(b)

only provides technology or infrastructure to a DAP or TCP operator
and does not possess digital tokens for or on behalf of the operator’s
clients. This includes a technology provider participating in a multiparty computation (MPC) arrangement that, under the anticipated
regulations (subject to Government processes), is not taken to possess
the digital tokens;

(c)

is a TCP, managed investment scheme or other financial product: see
RG 000.49–RG 000.54; or

(d)

is regulated as a financial market or CS facility: see RG 000.60.

If a facility meets both the DAP and TCP definitions, it is treated as a TCP
and not a DAP: see s761GC(4).

If an operator appoints an agent
RG 000.43

If an operator appoints an agent or other person to perform functions on its
behalf, the operator’s facility cannot avoid being a DAP or TCP. This is
because the operator is taken to have done, or failed to have done, anything
done, or not done, by the agent or other person because of the appointment:
s761GE. The operator therefore remains responsible for functions performed
through its agents (including any sub-agents) or other person engaged and
cannot avoid the application of the DAP or TCP framework by appointing
another person to perform those functions.
Note: See the Explanatory Memorandum for examples of matters for which the operator
remains responsible when functions are performed through an agent.

RG 000.44

This provision, combined with the concept of a joint issuer (see
paragraph 1.144 of the Explanatory Memorandum), works as an antiavoidance measure for the definitions of DAPs and TCPs. A facility that
provides a trading functionality (e.g. would be subject to the transactional
and settlement standards discussed below) cannot avoid being a DAP
because the entity outsources the asset-holding function to a third party.

Do other aspects of the financial services laws apply?
RG 000.45

An arrangement that meets the DAP or TCP definition may also have
interactions with other parts of the financial services laws. The Corporations
Act contains rules and exclusions that determine how the different parts of
the Corporations Act apply.

Digital tokens and underlying assets that are financial
products
RG 000.46

A digital token possessed through a DAP or an underlying asset held through
a TCP may be, or represent, a financial product; see INFO 225.

RG 000.47

The DAP or TCP is a separate financial product. Financial services
obligations may therefore apply both to services provided in relation to the
platform and to services provided in relation to a digital asset or underlying
asset that is itself a financial product: see Section C.

RG 000.48

A digital token created under a TCP may also itself be a financial product or
represent an interest in a financial product. In assessing whether that digital
asset is a financial product, you need to consider the rights and features of
the arrangement as outlined in INFO 225; but the redemption right is
disregarded (as by s765E). This typically means that if the underlying asset:
(a)

is not a financial product—the digital token created will not be a
financial product, unless holding the digital token that the TCP created
has some other rights or benefits in addition to the typical rights of the
underlying asset; and

(b)

is a financial product—the digital token created under the TCP will be
the same type of financial product as the underlying asset, unless
holding the digital token that the TCP created has some other rights or
benefits in addition to the typical rights of the underlying asset.

Managed investment schemes
RG 000.49

A DAP or TCP may also have features of a managed investment scheme,
including where assets are pooled or held as part of a common arrangement.

RG 000.50

Unless exclusions apply, if a facility meets the definition of a DAP or TCP
as well as the general definition of a managed investment scheme in s9, it
will be regulated as a managed investment scheme rather than as a DAP or
TCP: see s764A(1)(la)–(lb).
Note: If it is a managed investment scheme it may need to be registered under Ch 5C
and the operator may need to obtain the relevant AFS licence authorisation to operate a
scheme.

Exclusions
RG 000.51

Despite RG 000.49–RG 000.50, paragraphs (mc) and (md) of the definition
of ‘managed investment scheme’ in s9 exclude certain facilities that would
meet the definition of a DAP and TCP from being a managed investment
scheme. The exclusions apply to both DAPs and TCPs where:
(a)

clients have a right to redeem, or direct the delivery of, the underlying
assets;

(b)

the operator acts only on client instructions when making decisions
about the acquisition, disposal or use of those assets; and

(c)

the operator cannot materially negotiate or determine clients’ rights
relating to the underlying assets.

RG 000.52

For a TCP, the exclusion also requires the underlying assets to all belong to
the same class of asset and limits the divisibility of each digital token to the
extent that the corresponding underlying asset can reasonably be divided,
and each part physically delivered: see RG 000.35(c).

RG 000.53

These exclusions recognise that any features that might otherwise cause
these platforms to be managed investment schemes are generally incidental
to the platform services. They distinguish platforms where clients retain
rights over identifiable and deliverable assets from arrangements in which
the operator manages the assets (e.g. making investment allocation
decisions) or materially determines clients’ rights.
Note: See the Explanatory Memorandum for further explanation and examples of the
interaction between the DAP and TCP framework and the managed investment scheme
provisions.

RG 000.54

If the relevant exclusion does not apply, the arrangement must be assessed
against the general definition of a managed investment scheme in s9. It does
not become a managed investment scheme merely because it does not satisfy
the exclusions.
Note: See INFO 225 for guidance on when an arrangement involving digital assets may

Other financial products
RG 000.55

Some facilities may resemble DAPs or TCPs but instead constitute another
financial product. A relevant consideration is whether the facility requires
the operator to ensure that the underlying assets are dealt with on the
instructions of the client or the client’s nominee: see s761GC(1)(b)(ii) and
s761GD(1)(d)(ii).

RG 000.56

A facility may not be a DAP or TCP where the client cannot direct how the
underlying assets are dealt with. This could include where the client only has
a contractual right to receive money or another benefit calculated by
reference to those assets (e.g. cannot direct redemption of the assets).
Depending on its terms, the facility may be a derivative, a facility for making
a financial investment or another financial product.

RG 000.57

If the facility is a derivative, or a facility for making a financial investment
or another financial product, a person providing financial services in relation
to it may need an AFS licence with the relevant authorisations (unless an
exemption applies).

Custodial staking arrangements
RG 000.58

A custodial staking arrangement allows a beneficiary under a DAP or TCP
to authorise the operator to use underlying assets in validation or consensus
activities and receive the resulting rewards, after fees and charges: see s9F.

RG 000.59

Where the platform issuer holds an AFS licence authorising it to issue the
DAP or TCP, the part of the platform relating to the custodial staking
arrangement is not treated as a separate managed investment scheme or
financial product: see paragraph (me) of the definition of managed
investment scheme in s9 and s765A(1)(pa)–(pb). Therefore, the issuer does
not need a separate authorisation to operate a managed investment scheme
solely because it offers that custodial staking arrangement.

RG 000.60

If a facility could meet the definition of both a DAP and a financial market
or CS facility, the financial market definition prevails. The facility will not
be a DAP ‘to the extent that the facility is a financial market’: see s762B and
765A(1)(l)(i)–(ii).

RG 000.71

A DAP or TCP is a financial product, separate from any digital token or
underlying asset made available or held through it. A digital token possessed

RG 000.81

that apply to DAP and TCP operators. A limited exemption may apply to

Specific obligations for DAP and TCP operators

Key points
DAP and TCP operators must maintain adequate platform rules. Operators
that issue a DAP or TCP to retail clients must also provide those clients
with a DAP/TCP Guide and maintain a voting policy.
Operators must also comply with the asset-holding standards and, where
applicable, the transactional and settlement standards.
These requirements apply in addition to the AFS licensee obligations
outlined in Section D.

Overview of operator obligations
RG 000.104

Section D outlines the obligations applying to all AFS licensees. This section
explains the additional obligations applying to operators authorised to issue
a DAP or TCP.

RG 000.105

platform rules;

extent those standards relate to the financial services provided.

financial services provided and whether the relevant standards apply to those
services.

The underlying assets of a TCP can be any asset
(including digital tokens) except money, where the TCP
has created a digital token that confers the right to
redeem the token for the underlying asset or to direct
delivery of the underlying asset
